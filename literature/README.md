# Literature

This folder holds the papers that the job market paper draws on or that are under consideration for citation. Each file is named after its key in references.bib so that the PDF, the bib entry, and the citation in main_JMP.tex can be matched at a glance. When a paper is added, name the file after its bib key, add a row to the table below, and note whether it is already cited in the paper.

| File | Reference | Bib key | Cited | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ang2011_hedge_fund_leverage.pdf | Ang, Gorovyy and van Inwegen (2011), Hedge fund leverage, Journal of Financial Economics | ang2011 | yes | Hedge fund leverage rises when asset values increase and funding costs fall. This supports the expansion side of the framework. |
| alfaro2024_lash_risk_and_interest_rates.pdf | Alfaro, Bahaj, Czech, Hazell and Neamtu (2024), LASH risk and interest rates, NBER Working Paper 33241 | alfaro2024 (proposed, not yet in the bib file) | no | Solvency hedging by UK pension funds creates liquidity risk and forced selling when rates rise. This relates to the constraining regime and the margin mechanism. |
| jansen2025_long_term_investors_demand_shifts_and_yields.pdf | Jansen (2025), Long-term investors, demand shifts, and yields, Review of Financial Studies 38(1) | jansen2025 | no | Demand shifts by pension funds and insurers after a Dutch discount curve reform move the long end of the yield curve. This fits the demand and supply strand of the literature review. |
| jansen2026_pension_liquidity_risk.pdf | Jansen, Klingler, Ranaldo and Duijm (2026), Pension liquidity risk, Review of Financial Studies, forthcoming | jansen2026 | no | Margin calls on swap hedges force Dutch pension funds to sell government bonds procyclically. This is the same margin mechanism as the constraining regime, applied to a different investor. |
| jansen2024_granular_treasury_demand_with_arbitrageurs.pdf | Jansen, Li and Schmid (2024, revised June 2026), Granular Treasury demand with arbitrageurs, NBER Working Paper 33243 | jansen2024 | no | A demand system for Treasuries with risk averse arbitrageurs who absorb demand imbalances. Elasticity declines steeply with maturity and monetary tightening raises term premia through the arbitrageurs. |

The discussion slides from the EFA Doctoral Tutorial and the EFA version of the paper stay in the root folder because they are not literature.
